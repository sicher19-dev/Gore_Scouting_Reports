The Briefing

Five things. If nothing else survives the flight west, these do.

1

This is the chase team of the season — the at-bats end off the plate. Siri expands at 46–61% depending on the pitch. Peraza chases everything soft (42–47%) and has walked three times in 102 trips. Guzman chases changeups at 56%, d'Arnaud at 56%, Heineman at 53%. Outside of Trout, Neto and Grissom, nobody makes you throw a strike. The changeup and curve below the zone feed all night.

2

The Trout door is the curveball — and it's wide open. .879 OPS, a .422 on-base, 22 walks: he's the disciplined red box. But against lefty curves he whiffs 43%, chases a career-unlike 27%, and manages .112 expected damage at 74 EV — the bat literally slows to 70.0 on them. Every other pitch class he punishes (.448 to .516). The best hitter in the lineup has exactly one soft spot, and it's the pitch this series has been rationing. Against Trout, the ration lifts.

3

Neto swings the steepest path these reports have ever graded. 19.9° average — 14.8° even against fastballs, 24–25° at everything soft. The consequences: 40% whiff on changeups, 41% on slide-shape, 50% on curves, and ride above the zone beats the uphill plane. But hanging spin gets launched (.526 on slide-shape, 5 HR). Below the knees or above the letters — the leadoff at-bat lives at the extremes.

4

Two of July 8's tormentors are gone; one remains. Adell (both homers that night) and O'Hoppe were traded at the deadline. Vaughn Grissom — three hits at 97–107 EV in that game — is still here, hitting cleanup-adjacent, and still dangerous on zone fastballs (.444, 95.0 EV). He's the rematch grudge that matters: curveballs (33% whiff, .171) and changeups away, nothing straight in the white.

5

The bottom of the order is a strikeout harvest. Siri (37% K rate vs lefties, chases everything), Guzman (.150, 45% whiff on changeups AND 45% on elevated fastballs), plus whichever of Meckler (60% whiff on elevated heat), Heineman (64.6 bat, chops at −3.7°) or Josh Lowe (52% whiff on lefty sliders — same hole he had in Tampa) fills out the nine. Slots 5 through 9 should produce five-plus strikeouts on soft stuff below and ride above.

The Rematch — July 8, Graded

Five innings, eight hits, five runs. Run the tape backwards through everything learned since, and that start becomes the clearest before-picture of the season. Every fix that's landed in the last month maps to a specific wound from that night.

1

The cutter did the heavy damage — twelve thrown, and the ledger reads like a warning label. Neto's 109.7 EV double. Grissom's 106.9 EV single. A 109.5 EV rocket from O'Hoppe that found a glove. The Trout walk. That was the season's cutter problem at full volume. Since then: one, three, one across the last three starts, and this series' recommendation stands at zero. The Angels have not seen the version of this arsenal without it.

2

The zone-curve debt actually got collected that night. Neto doubled a hanging curve (zone 4). Grissom singled one at 97 EV. Siri singled another. Three curves that caught plate, three hits — the bill this series keeps warning about was paid in Anaheim, by this exact lineup. The fix is unchanged and now has receipts with these hitters' names on them: below the knees, or to the two hitters this report explicitly licenses (Trout, and nobody else in the zone).

3

The changeup owned them even in the loss. Five strikeouts finished on it that night — Neto, Siri, O'Hoppe, Peraza twice — in a start where everything else leaked. It was 26% usage then; it's a credited trend at 28–33% now. Same pitch, bigger role, against the lineup that already proved it can't touch it.

4

What's different in a month: everything the reverse reports tracked. Slider zone rate vs lefties 50% then, 20% now. Changeup 26% then, three straight starts on target since. Cutter twelve then, effectively shelved now. The last two starts: 13 innings, 3 runs, net run value −2.90 combined. The Angels are facing the August version with the July scouting report. Their book on him is out of date; this one isn't.

Game Plan

The whole start in six calls.

Expand

This lineup gets one strike an at-bat, then leaves the zone. Siri, Peraza, Guzman, d'Arnaud, Heineman and Ballesteros combine for the heaviest chase rates of the series and almost no walks. Strike one however it comes; everything after finishes below the knees or above the letters. The Angels will donate ten outs off the plate if the plan has the patience to collect them.

Red Box

The Trout rule: curveball-first, without apology. One door — 43% whiff, .112 damage, a bat that decelerates only for this pitch — and it can take the bottom of the zone, not just below it. Everything else waits for a mistake that never has to come. If he walks, he walks; a .422 on-base means he was reaching base anyway. Three curve-driven at-bats to the best hitter in the park is the plan working, whatever the outcome.

Tunnel

The FF/CH tunnel harvests the right side. Guzman (45% whiff both ends — late on ride, over the fade), d'Arnaud (56% chase), Siri, Peraza if he starts, Neto at the top. The changeup already struck out five Angels in the July loss at 26% usage; it arrives tonight as a credited three-start trend. Hold 25–30% and let the pitch that owns this lineup do its work.

Receipts

The July 8 wounds have names — don't reopen them. Zero cutters (three of that night's four loudest balls). Zone curves only to Trout (three zone curves, three hits last time — Neto, Grissom, Siri all cashed one). And Grissom sees nothing straight in the white: his three-hit night lived entirely on mislocated hard stuff to a dead-flat path. The rematch is won by subtraction.

Command

The fastball address book is the one open item. San Francisco's entire damage ledger — three walks, three hits — was wandering four-seams in innings two through four, with the velocity fine. Tonight's respect list for the heater (Peraza, Grissom, Trout, Schanuel's patience) makes the same drift costlier. The mid-game check-in is a real thing now: if the release wanders, the curve and changeup carry innings until it returns.

Park

Angel Stadium notes: 330 corners, big alleys, heavy night air. The marine layer knocks down fly balls after dark — the ladder plan and the pop-up outs play up at 6:38 local. The short corners are Neto's and Siri's pull-side reward for hanging spin, which is one more argument for the season's oldest rule: soft stuff finishes down, or it doesn't get thrown.
