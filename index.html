The Briefing

Five things. If nothing else survives batting practice, these do.

1

The strikeout budget is the two biggest names. Rafael Devers is hitting .188 against lefties with 44 strikeouts in 150 trips — and the mechanism is a gift: 44% whiff on lefty four-seams, 48% when it's elevated. Willy Adames is at .182 with the steepest swing in the lineup (10.7° at fastballs, an 8.2-foot arc). The ride four-seam at the top is a strikeout machine against both. Between them: eight to ten free strikeouts are on the table.

2

The Devers fine print: miss big or hit big. When he does connect with a lefty four-seam it comes back at .560 expected damage and 94 mph — and Globe Life's right field is 326. The whiff math wins the bet (nearly half his swings miss), but the ladder has to be honest: top rail and above, never the 2.8–3.2 shelf. A mislocated "up" to Devers is the most expensive mistake on the card.

3

The real threats aren't the names — they're Ramos and Susac. Heliot Ramos (.861) does damage on every single pitch class he touches: .527 on four-seams, .616 on slide-shape, .627 on curves, at 96–99 EV. Nothing is safe in the zone. Daniel Susac (.970, hot) is the opposite: huge soft-stuff holes — 44% whiff on changeups, 41% on sliders, 40%+ chase on both. One gets nothing in the white; the other gets nothing but soft stuff below it.

4

Bryce Eldridge inverts the usual rules. Do NOT throw him fastballs: 4% whiff, zero misses on elevated heat, .597 expected damage at 95.7 EV — he covers the entire hard-stuff plane. But breaking balls eat him alive: 43% whiff on curves, 31% on sliders — and he barely chases (5% on curves). Which means the curveball gets thrown FOR STRIKES to him: the one hitter on the schedule where the zone-curve ban lifts.

5

Arraez and Lee can't hurt you — stop trying to beat them. Luis Arraez swings 61.7 mph — the slowest bat these reports have ever graded — with a 5.8-foot swing and near-zero whiffs. Jung Hoo Lee is at 66.4, dead flat. Neither can drive the ball (78–88 EV everywhere). Fill the zone, take the outs the defense gives, and spend zero craft pitches. The at-bats that need art are slots 2, 3, 4 and 7 — not these two.

Carryover From Tampa Bay

The reverse report graded it: net run value −0.57, with all three damage swings on pre-identified banned pitches and eighteen of the last nineteen retired. Here's what carries forward.

1

Hold the changeup at 25–30% — one more start makes it a trend. It hit 28% in Tampa Bay for the first time on target, and finished four of the nine strikeouts. Tonight's customers: Susac (44% whiff, 47% chase), Adames (45% chase), Ramos (47% chase — but chase only, .465 on contact), and Devers in small doses (54% chase). The featured pitch stays featured.

2

The cutter is a two-start damage pattern. Target: zero, third report running. Three thrown in Tampa Bay produced the Aranda double (101.5 EV) and the Williamson HBP. Seattle before that. The season number is .486. It's an offseason-lab project now, not a game pitch — and Ramos (.616 on cut/slide shapes) is waiting for it.

3

Zone breaking balls survived another night unpunished — the bill hasn't come, the debt is real. Curves caught the zone at 44% and sliders at 43% in Tampa Bay without damage. San Francisco is a worse audience for that luck: Ramos runs .627 on zone curves, Devers .478. The Eldridge exception (card 4 in the Briefing) is exactly that — an exception, for one hitter, on one pitch.

4

The first-inning lesson travels whole. Every run in Tampa Bay came from three first-inning pitches, all three banned by address in the scout. The first time through this lineup runs Arraez–Ramos–Eldridge–Devers: a zone-filler, a chase-only threat, a no-fastball hitter, and a ladder strikeout. Four different plans in four batters, first inning. Walk out of the first clean and the start sets up exactly like Wednesday's innings two through seven.

Game Plan

The whole start in six calls.

Budget

Devers and Adames are the strikeout budget — collect it with the ladder. A combined 75 strikeouts against lefties between them, both beaten the same way: ride four-seams at the top rail (44–48% and 38% whiff up), finished with spin down. Six honest at-bats, eight potential strikeouts, and the season's best pitch doing the season's clearest work.

Red Box

The Ramos rule: nothing in the white, and the walk is a feature. He damages all five pitch classes in the zone at 96+ EV. Chase changeups away, ladder above with two strikes, and hand him first base without guilt — the two hitters behind him are hitting .250 and .188 against lefties. Do not let the 2-hole beat you when the middle of the order is the strikeout budget.

Invert

The Eldridge exception: spin for strikes, fastballs never. The one at-bat where the season's rules flip — zone curveballs are the weapon (43% whiff, no chase) and the ride four-seam is banned (zero elevated whiffs, .597 damage). Open with the curve for a called strike, stay with spin, and resist every instinct to establish the heater.

Surrender

Concede contact to Arraez, Lee and Koss — on purpose, early, cheaply. Three flat, slow swings that can't miss and can't hurt (62–67 mph bats). Zone sinkers and fastballs, defense does the rest, pitch count stays flat. The craft budget goes to slots 2, 3, 4, 6 and 7. Roughly twelve at-bats tonight should cost three pitches or fewer.

Discipline

The Tampa Bay first inning is the whole lesson. Three banned addresses, three runs. Tonight's first inning stacks four different plans in four hitters — zone-fill, chase-only, curve-for-strikes, ladder — and the transitions are where addresses slip. Slow down between them. And the standing items hold: zero cutters (Ramos is the collector at .616), changeup at 25–30%, sliders finishing six inches off the plate.

Park

Globe Life notes: roof likely closed, 326 to right, dead center plays big. The right-field line is Devers' and Eldridge's pull side — the mislocated "up" that stays on the shelf is a short-porch souvenir, which is why the ladder commits fully or not at all. Straightaway center (407) forgives the flat-swing liners; let Arraez and Lee hit it there all night.
