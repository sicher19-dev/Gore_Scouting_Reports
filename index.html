The Briefing

Five things. Written for 94.5 — anything more is a bonus, never a plan.

1

This is the first scout of the series where the ladder is NOT the plan — because this lineup has no ladder holes. Nobody in the projected nine clears a 30% whiff rate on elevated fastballs; Simpson (2.5%), Yandy (16.9%) and Fortes (14.6%) barely swing through anything up. They are a flat-plane contact team — 2.3°, 3.3°, 3.2°, 3.8° paths at the top of the order. At 94.5 that's a gift: the fastball works on edges for called strikes and the soft stuff does the finishing. The September blueprint from the A's II reverse — curve-heavy, fade-heavy, honest gauges — meets the lineup it was drawn for.

2

Junior Caminero swings the fastest bat in nine reports (79.4 mph) and gets nothing straight, ever. .949 with 8 homers, .573 on zone four-seams at 96.4 EV, .469 on sinkers at 101.9, .520 on curves. He also walks (29) and doesn't chase (25%) — so there's no cheap out. There IS a door: the changeup (24.7% whiff, .367 — his lowest damage class), which is exactly how the July strikeout happened. Fades below and away, sliders off the plate for effect, and the walk accepted without a flinch. A strike he can reach is the worst outcome on the board.

3

The changeup is a near-universal door — with exactly one hard ban. Yandy chases it at 40% (his only real expansion), DeLuca at 54% (.187), Fortes at 43%, Mateo at 40%, Walls whiffs 37%, Aranda 33%, Caminero 25% — four of July's nine strikeouts came on it. The ban is Ryan Vilade: .484 on changeups, and the 107.8 EV homer on July 29 was a zone changeup at the knees. To him it's fastballs on edges and sliders off the plate (46% chase). Nobody else in the lineup gets the changeup withheld.

4

The walk thread meets an aggressive bottom half — spend it. Eight walks in the last two starts is the live problem. This lineup's 6-through-9 chases 36–44% of pitches off the plate (Mateo 44%, DeLuca 39%, Fortes 36%, Simpson 37% with three walks in 173 trips). Early strikes to the bottom are nearly guaranteed swings, which means the count discipline the reverse asked for gets its easiest possible test. The patient ones — Caminero (19% first-pitch swing), Aranda (24% chase), Walls (23%) — are where the free bases hide; the rest of the card is attack, attack, attack.

5

The July receipts: three damage swings, three retired addresses. Yandy's homer came on a fastball at the bottom-middle of the zone. Aranda's double came on the cutter — extinct. Vilade's homer came on a zone changeup — now banned to him specifically. Everything else that night was nine strikeouts and zero walks, with the sinker producing three of them to lefties (Aranda twice, Simpson). Same lineup, same plan — the three pitches that scored no longer exist in this matchup.

The Rematch — July 29, Graded and Answered

The Tampa start was the first of the run: 7 innings, 3 runs, 9 strikeouts, 0 walks. Its reverse report found that every run came on a pitch that would soon be banned. Five weeks later, here's what carries and what changed.

1

The three damage swings are three closed doors. Yandy Díaz homered on a fastball at the bottom-middle — the heart-of-plate thread that was finally retired last start (one middle fastball in 95 pitches vs the A's). Jonathan Aranda doubled on a cutter — the pitch is extinct, with its two-pitch cameo last start ending on ball four as a reminder why. Ryan Vilade homered on a zone changeup — his card now bans the pitch class outright. The rematch begins with their July offense already subtracted.

2

What worked travels — and it was the September blueprint before there was one. Nine strikeouts with zero walks: four on changeups (Caminero, DeLuca, Walls, Williamson), three on sinkers to lefties (Aranda twice, Simpson), two on fastballs. The changeup hit 20% for the first time that night and has never fallen below 15% since. The sinker had its best night of the season here — a whitelist entry with receipts, not just numbers.

3

What changed: the arm, the roster, and the setting. July 29 was a 95.0 night with 95.4 in the first; Sunday projects to 94.5 — so this report writes edges-and-soft where July wrote ladders (and July barely used ladders against this flat-swing lineup anyway). Williamson (two strikeouts that night) is gone; Jorge Mateo (44% chase, .790 on sinkers in a small sample) is now an everyday middle-order bat vs lefties. Aranda has sat two of the last four lefty games — if he's out, Vilade moves to second and the lineup gets one more right-handed bat with a changeup door.

4

The Caminero precedent stands: the off-plan win that became the plan. The July reverse graded his strikeout as a battle won off-card — the scout said fastballs up, the dugout went changeup, and he swung through it. His refreshed book confirms the dugout: 24.7% whiff on changeups, .367 damage — his weakest class by a wide margin — against .573 on the heat the card originally recommended. The card learned. This time the changeup IS the plan.

Game Plan

The whole start in six calls — written for 94.5, and for a lineup that fits it.

Setting

The gauge is a setting: plan for 94.5 from pitch one and treat anything more as found money. No first-inning test this time — the last reverse retired it. The fastball is a location pitch today: edges for strike one, just-off-the-plate chase to the aggressive bottom half, finisher only to the bench ladder holes. Soft-first sequencing is the default. The A's II blueprint (20% curves, 19% changeups, one middle fastball in 95 pitches) is the model, not the exception.

Fade

The changeup is the whole card — 30% or more, below the thigh, to everyone but Vilade. Four of July's nine strikeouts; DeLuca 54% chase, Yandy 40%, Fortes 43%, Mateo 40%, Walls 37% whiff, Caminero 25% whiff at his lowest damage. The Serven and Doyle letters-singles are the standing height receipts: nothing above the thigh, ever. Vilade's .484 and July homer are the one exemption — his at-bat is fastballs on edges and sliders off the plate, the only heat-first plan of the day.

Caminero

The fastest bat in nine reports gets the softest diet and an accepted walk. .573 on zone heat, .469 on sinkers at 102 EV, .520 on curves — nothing straight, nothing spinning up, ever. The changeup (25% whiff, .367) is the door the dugout found in July against the card's advice; now it IS the card. First base three times is a fine box score line. A strike he can reach is not.

Counts

Eight walks in two starts meets the most exploitable bottom half of the season — fix the thread here. Mateo chases 60% of fastballs off the plate. Fortes 46%. Simpson has three walks in 173 trips. DeLuca chases 39% overall. Strike one to the bottom five is nearly a guaranteed swing, and nothing in the zone is required to retire any of them. The free bases that cost innings in Chicago and Arlington live only at the top: Caminero (19% first-pitch swing), Aranda (24% chase), Walls (23%). Budget for those; refuse all others.

Spin

The slider comes back and the curve splits by handedness. Three sliders against the A's was a season low; five or six lefties in this lineup want it (Mullins .107, Simpson .243, Aranda 24%, Palacios 37%) — and Vilade's 46% chase makes it his chase pitch off the plate. The curve is a free strike to the lefty contact bats (Simpson 7% chase, Walls takes it, Yandy takes it early) and forbidden to the right-handed power (Vilade .554, Caminero .520, Hicks .569). Whitelist rides at six straight: Mateo .790, Caminero .469, Yandy .403 and DeLuca .367 never see a sinker; Aranda and Simpson do, with July's three strikeouts as the receipt.

Matinee

Sunday day game under the roof — the routine changes, the standard doesn't. A 1:35 start is the first afternoon look of the series; velocity often reads a tick different in day games, and the gauge gets read as information, not as a trigger. The lineup's counterweight to a dome: nine flat swings that put everything in play means the defense works more than the radar does. Three straight homerless starts (22 innings) against three lineups with real power — this one has eight homers apiece from Yandy and Caminero and almost nothing else. Edges, fades, and zero gifts. The July script, minus the three pitches that scored.
