The Briefing

Five things. If nothing else survives the clubhouse door, these do.

1

The reverse split is real, and Seattle built their lineup for it. Lefties are hitting .280 with a .794 OPS this year; righties are at .223. The reason is mechanical: Seattle's left-handed bats — Crawford, Young, Naylor — swing nearly flat against fastballs (0.8°, 2.4°, 3.0° attack angles) and simply don't miss them. Against the lefty stack, this is a contact-management night, not a strikeout night.

2

Shelve the cutter. .486 expected damage on contact and 4 of the 14 homers allowed — in only 168 pitches. It's the single biggest leak in the season line. Seattle's flat, zone-covering swings are the worst possible audience for 90 mph with small cut. If it appears at all, it's buried at the back foot of a righty — never for a strike.

3

The four-seam up is a targeted weapon, not a blanket one. 16.3 inches of ride at 95+ is genuinely elite carry — but half this lineup swings flat enough to cover it. Ladder it to the steep swings: Raleigh (35% whiff up), Emerson (75%), Garver (42%), Wilson, Canzone (67% above the zone). Skip it to the flat swings: Crawford, Young, Naylor, Arozarena.

4

Julio Rodríguez is the game. .857 OPS and 7 of the team's homers against lefties, with the fastest bat on their roster (76 mph) swinging dead flat at heaters. There is no zone pitch that beats him. But he chases curveballs below the zone at 58% and changeups off the edge at 45%. Chase-only plan — and know that a curve that catches white comes back at .783 expected damage. Below the knees or not at all.

5

Cal Raleigh's right side is broken — attack him like an out. .143/.244/.221 against lefties this year in 90 trips. He's swinging 17° uphill under everything: 35% whiff on elevated four-seams, 45% on curveballs. The name on the back says MVP votes; the swing says 8-hole. Ride up, curve down, next hitter.

Season Overview

Twenty-one starts, no missed time, velocity rock-steady all year. The 4.80 isn't a stuff problem — it's two fixable leaks: the cutter, and breaking balls left in the zone. Since June 1 the wOBA against has climbed from .289 to .348 on exactly those pitches.

Game Plan

The whole start in six calls.

North-South

The identity is vertical: 16 inches of ride up, 11 inches of depth down. The four-seam/curveball pair separates by nearly 28 vertical inches out of the same tunnel. Against Seattle's steep swings — Raleigh, Wilson, Garver, Canzone — the ladder up and the curve under it is the whole at-bat.

Shelve

No cutters tonight. .486 damage, a homer every 42 thrown, and a lineup of flat, zone-covering swings waiting for exactly that shape at exactly that speed. The changeup takes its pitches: it's the highest-whiff, lowest-damage offering in the bag and Seattle's right-handed bench (Garver 50% whiff, Robles 54%, Wilson 39%) is built to miss it.

Red Box

The Julio rule: nothing in the zone, ever. Every zone pitch class returns .400+ expected damage against him. The at-bat is curveballs below the knees (58% chase), changeups dying off the edge, and top-rail fastballs with two strikes. A walk is a good outcome in all three trips. Do not let the 2-hole beat you when the 4-hole is hitting .143.

Contact

Against the lefty stack, manage contact — don't chase strikeouts. Crawford, Young and Naylor swing 0.8–3.0° flat at fastballs and won't miss them; they also can't damage them (68–70 mph bats, sub-.370 slugs). Sinkers at the knees and strikes early turn them into ground balls. Save the slider for two strikes — Naylor 36%, Young 35%, and Raley off the bench at 64%.

Zone Disc.

Know who takes and who chases. Crawford (9% chase) and Canzone (9 walks) win nibbling contests — attack them in the zone. Julio, Robles (52% chase, 0 walks), Raleigh (41% chase) and Arozarena expand — make them. The free passes that fueled the June slump go to the wrong list.

Finish

Emerson and Raleigh are the strikeout budget. A 75% elevated-fastball whiff rate in the 9-hole and a .143 cleanup hitter swinging 17° uphill: between them, five or six strikeouts are sitting there without spending anything risky. Take the free ones so the tough outs — Julio, Arozarena, Canzone — can be pitched around the edges without pressure.
