The Briefing

Five things. The third date with the same lineup rewards whoever adjusted last.

1

The head-to-head book is complete, and it says one sentence: soft below wins, spin in the zone loses. Across two games, the changeup below the zone has ended at least eight Angels at-bats by strikeout — Neto punched out on the identical down-away changeup in BOTH games, Siri twice, Peraza twice, d'Arnaud, Grissom. Meanwhile all six of their breaking-ball damage events came on curves and sliders that caught plate. This lineup has never solved the executed version of the plan. Don't offer the other version.

2

Trout, third edition: the license is revoked, the respect is total. Anaheim's licensed zone-bottom curve left at 101.5 — and it lives in his numbers now (.287 on curves, up from .112). The curve is still his weakest pitch class (39% whiff), but it works below the zone only. His two walks against Gore both came on cutters in the identical spot; that pitch no longer exists. What remains: ladder four-seams at the top rail with two strikes (24% whiff up, 7% chase — he won't expand, so it must be honest), curves buried, and a .422-caliber walk accepted without flinching. He's on a 24-walk pace vs lefties; the free base was always part of his price.

3

Grissom is 4-for-6 lifetime against this arsenal — and every hit came on a pitch in the zone. 105.2, 106.9, 97.0, plus a flare — all in the white (z4, z8, z9). His two strikeouts? A ladder fastball above the letters and a changeup below the knees. The book writes itself: the man taxes the box and loses the extremes. Nothing between the knees and the letters, all night, no exceptions.

4

The strikeout harvest runs through the same doors as last time — they've gotten bigger. Neto's changeup whiff is up to 45.5% with the steepest path yet graded (20.0°). Siri (28 K in 75 PA now) chases curves at 64% and changeups at 54%. Guzman whiffs on 51% of changeups. d'Arnaud chases the fade at a comical 67%. Peraza expands at 47%. Ten-plus strikeouts are on the card if the soft stuff stays under the zone.

5

The fuel gauge reads full — six days of rest answers Sacramento's question. The 94.6 came on a fifth start in 23 days; the shapes held even then (16.6 inches of ride). Friday arrives on the longest rest since the All-Star break. First-inning readings still get checked — that habit stays — but the expectation is the 95.4–95.7 band, and with it, the full plan. The low-velo gear stays printed on the last page, unneeded.

Six zone breaking balls produced Neto's other double, Siri's single, Grissom's third hit, Peraza's single, and the Trout homer. The zone curve is the last door left open — and no opponent has walked through it more often.

2

Nine strikeouts in 5.1 innings — Siri three times, Grissom twice at the vertical extremes, Neto, Schanuel, Ballesteros, d'Arnaud — with the changeup finishing four and 30% usage on target. The lineup's answer was one swing on one mislocated curve. That's the whole gap between the July version and the August version: five runs became one.

3

Individual receipts worth carrying to the mound: Neto has struck out on the same down-away changeup in consecutive meetings and still hasn't touched one below the zone. Siri is 4 strikeouts in 6 PAs when the finish pitch stays out of the white — his only contact against Gore is two zone curves. Ballesteros saw ten pitches in Anaheim and whiffed on three of the four sliders. Peraza's lone hit was the non-licensed zone curve; his three whiff-pitches are all changeups. The pattern repeats at every name: they hit what catches plate, they miss what doesn't.

4

What's different for game three: the venue, the rest, and the lineup's shape. Globe Life instead of the marine layer — elevated mistakes travel further here, which raises the price of a dishonest ladder. Six days of rest instead of a five-starts-in-23-days sprint. And the Angels' recent lefty lineups have promoted Siri into the 4–5 spots while resting Schanuel — the strikeout harvest may bat higher than the scout below projects. Every version of their nine leads with the same two names, and the plan for those two is settled law by now.

Game Plan

The whole start in six calls — the third one against this team, written from evidence instead of projection.

Last Door

Their entire remaining offense against this arsenal is the zone breaking ball — close the door. Six zone-spin receipts in two games: Neto, Siri twice, Grissom, Peraza, Trout. The cutter that fed everything else is already gone. Every curve and slider below the zone in the trilogy has produced strikeouts and weak chases; every one that caught plate is in their highlight reel. This is the simplest game plan of the season: the same pitches, six inches lower, and their run production has no source left.

Harvest

The changeup below the zone has nine strikeouts of this team in two games — run it until they adjust. Neto (identical K both games), Siri, Peraza, d'Arnaud, Grissom, Schanuel have all gone down on it. The season numbers say the holes grew: Guzman 51%, Neto 45.5%, d'Arnaud 67% chase. Hold 25–30%, address at the shoetops, and let the most proven weapon in the matchup collect double digits.

Trout V3

The honest version, nothing else: buried curves, top-rail ladders, and the walk as a budgeted expense. The cutter walked him twice in identical spots — gone. The zone-bottom license cost a run — revoked. His curve numbers still say it's the weakest door (.287, 39% whiff), strictly below the zone now. With a 24-walk pace, first base was always going to happen once; the plan's only failure mode is trying to prevent it.

Extremes

Grissom gets nothing between the knees and the letters — literally nothing. 4-for-6 lifetime, all four hits in the white; both strikeouts at the vertical extremes. His .479 season number on zone heat says the trilogy sample isn't a fluke. Ladder above, fade below, and if the count fills, the walk to a zero-homer hitter is cheaper than the single that always comes.

Fuel

Six days of rest answers Sacramento — verify it in the first, then spend it. The shapes never left (16.6 inches of ride at 94.6); the gas did. Opening readings at 95+ unlock the full ladder plan against a lineup with four hitters above 30% elevated whiff. The slider depth check matters too: 2.9 inches of ride is the eraser, 5.1 is the flare Meckler singled. Both gauges, first inning, then commit.

Park

Globe Life, trilogy edition: no marine layer to forgive the shelf, big center to swallow the steep swings. Anaheim's night air turned two loud Trout balls into outs; this roof does less charity. The counterweight: Neto (20.0°), Siri and Peraza's uppercuts send their mishits to the 407 alley. Honest ladders and buried spin — the same two sentences as every call above, because in the third game there are no surprises left. Just execution.
