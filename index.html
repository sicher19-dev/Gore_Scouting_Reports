The Briefing

Five things. If nothing else survives the reunion handshakes, these do.

1

Familiarity is their scouting report — recency is yours. Abrams, Ruiz, Young, Crews and Lile shared a clubhouse with this arsenal. What they remember is the pitcher who threw twelve cutters a night and hung breaking balls in the zone. That pitcher retired in July. The slider now lives off the plate, the changeup runs the right side at 25–30%, the cutter is gone entirely. Their book is a season out of date, and the gap between what they expect and what arrives is itself a weapon — especially early, first time through.

2

Brady House is the hottest hitter this series has ever graded — and his door is the changeup. .360/.415/.600 against lefties, and nothing hard is safe: .571 on four-seams, .628 on sinkers, both at 96 EV. But he whiffs on 47% of changeups and chases 43% of them, with a swing that collapses to 18.4° reaching for the fade. The identity pitch draws the marquee assignment. Hard stuff visits the edges; the changeup does the living.

3

You cannot throw a fastball past Keibert Ruiz. Literally. The man who caught every bullpen of the Washington years has zero whiffs on lefty four-seams, zero on sinkers, zero on elevated heat this season — 64 swings, no misses. He also chases 41% of fastballs and slaps them at 85.8 EV, so heat gets contact, not strikeouts. The pitch he never caught cleanly is the pitch that beats him: 43.5% whiff on changeups, .105 on curves. If he starts, soft stuff only; if he pinch-hits late, same sentence.

4

The Abrams at-bat is the fixed slider's biggest test — 247 pitches of evidence say it wins. The trade brother strikes out 30% of the time against lefties, whiffing 39% and chasing 46% on slide-shape (plus 47% whiff, 54% chase on curves). But six homers live in the hangs. The pitch that got fixed in San Francisco meets the hitter it was fixed for: down-and-in, finishing below his 17° reach, four times.

5

Chaparro's .662 on zone fastballs is the second-worst heat number of the series — and Jacob Young is quietly .861. Chaparro (5 HR) punishes anything straight in the white at 98.3 EV; he sees edges, ladders and sliders (44% whiff) only. Young's .861 is built on .578 fastball damage — the 6-hole is not a rest stop. The two "quiet" righty bats are where zone-heat discipline gets tested most.

Carryover From Sacramento

The ledger frowned at a winning line: +0.66 net run value, all four extra-base hits on fastballs, and a velocity reading that moved workload to the top of the watch list. Thursday comes on full rest. Here's what carries.

1

Velocity is watch item number one — the first inning is the test. 94.6 in Sacramento, under the band for the first time, on a fifth start in 23 days. Thursday arrives on a normal five-day reset. If the opening readings sit 95+, the sprint explains the dip and the full plan runs. If they open under 95 again, simplify: lean the changeup and slider harder, spend the heater only where the cards demand it, and let the six-inning version be enough. Watch, don't panic — but watch.

2

The zone curve, ninth verse — against the two worst possible audiences for the habit. Eight straight starts with a zone-curve receipt, the last one a 102 mph two-run homer. Crews whiffs 55% on curves that finish below the zone; House whiffs 50%. Both punish the ones that don't (House .479, Crews's damage concentrated on hangs). The pitch is a weapon below the knees and a batting-practice fastball above them. No licenses this week — nobody in this lineup freezes.

3

Middle-middle fastballs are a three-start thread; this lineup collects them. Three doubles from the heart of the zone Saturday, the SF walk cluster before that. Thursday's collectors: Chaparro (.662), House (.571), Young (.578), Abrams (.568). The release-consistency bullpen work matters more this week than any sequence on this card.

4

The wins travel: zero cutters, three straight perfect sinker whitelists — and the changeup share comes back. The first true zero-cutter start finally happened; the old teammates who remember that pitch never see it again. The sinker whitelist here: green for Nuñez (zero whiffs but zero damage, .224), Lile, Vivas and Chaparro; banned for House (.628) and careful with Tena. And the changeup returns to 25–30% with the deepest customer list yet: House 47%, Ruiz 43.5%, Abrams 38.5%, Chaparro 36%, Nuñez 33%.

Game Plan

The whole start in six calls.

New Man

Show them the pitcher they've never met — early, deliberately, first time through. Five of these hitters carry dugout-rail memories of the 2024–25 arsenal: cutters, zone curves, fastball-heavy sequences. The first look sets the correction: Abrams opens with sliders that finish, Ruiz (if he plays) opens with fades, nobody sees a cutter all night. Every early at-bat that contradicts their memory buys cheap strikes for the late innings.

Red Box

The House rule: the hottest bat of the series gets the softest diet of the season. A 1.015 OPS built entirely on velocity — .571 to .628 on everything hard — against a 47% changeup whiff. Fastballs visit the edges to set the tunnel and never linger; the fade does the living below his hands. His sinker ban (.628) is absolute. If he beats three changeup-led at-bats, tip the cap; do not let him beat a heater.

Brothers

The Abrams plan is one shape, four times: the fixed slider under his reach. A 39% whiff and 46% chase on 247 lefty slide-shapes — the most documented hole this series has found — belonging to the man who rode to Washington in the same trade. Down-and-in, below the 17° reach, at the 20% zone standard. The six homers in his hangs are the only counterargument; don't write it.

Zone Tax

The zone-heart is a toll road tonight — four collectors at .568 or better. Chaparro (.662), Young (.578), House (.571), Abrams (.568): the middle-middle fastball thread from the last three starts meets its worst audience. The release check-in from the bullpen work is live. And the ninth-verse rule stands: curves below the knees or not at all — Crews (55% whiff below) and House (50%) make it the strikeout pitch exactly as long as it never touches the shelf.

Fuel Gauge

The first-inning radar readings decide the night's shape. 94.6 last start after five outings in 23 days; Thursday comes on a full reset. At 95+, everything above runs as written. Under 95 again: shift weight to the changeup (deepest customer list of the year — House, Ruiz, Abrams, Chaparro, Nuñez all 33%+) and the slider, spend the heater only on the ladders the cards demand, and let six clean innings be the win. The plan has a low-velo gear; use it without ego.

Park

Globe Life, home edition: roof likely closed, 326 to right, forgiveness in center. Abrams's pull side is the short porch — one more reason his sliders finish down-and-in rather than down-and-middle. The flat-swing contact bats (Nuñez, Ford, Vivas) die in the big alleys; let them hit it there. Same building where the SF start's soft stuff was untouchable — the formula travels eight days forward.
