The Briefing

Five things. If nothing else survives the flight, these do.

1

The flare-band warning is doubled tonight. Tampa Bay swings flatter at fastballs than any lineup yet — Williamson chops at minus-2, Vilade and Yandy and Fortes and Simpson all live under three degrees. Flat swings don't miss zone fastballs and they don't top them either; they line them. The soft-contact-that-falls problem gets worse here unless the location discipline shows up. The white of the plate is rented, not owned.

2

The good news: most of those flat bats are slow. Simpson swings 63.8, Aranda 67.8, Walls 67.9, Mullins 68.5, Williamson 69.5 — bottom-shelf bat speed across two-thirds of the order. Soft contact is available everywhere except four bats. Strikes early to the slow group are fine; the damage lives entirely with Caminero, Vilade, Díaz and DeLuca.

3

This is the changeup's biggest night of the year. DeLuca chases it at 51%, Fortes whiffs 36% and chases 42%, Walls whiffs 35%, Williamson 29%, and Yandy chases it at 41%. Five of the nine projected hitters have a changeup-shaped hole. The 25–30% target against righties isn't aspirational tonight — it's the game plan.

4

Junior Caminero is the game. .995 OPS, 7 homers against lefties, and a 79.6 mph bat — the fastest swing either of these reports has ever graded. He damages every zone pitch: .558 on four-seams at 97.9 EV, .459 on sinkers at 100.7. One door exists: he chases curveballs below the zone at 44%. And he's drawn 22 walks — he'll take the free pass. Curveballs buried, changeups dying away, nothing in the white, and the walk is a win all three times.

5

The fastball map is nearly inverted from Seattle. Up works on exactly four bats: Mullins (32% whiff up), DeLuca (43% up — late, not under), Walls (25%) and Aranda with two strikes. The rest of the order covers the top of the zone with the barrel. Against the flat army, the four-seam is a location piece on edges — not a challenge pitch, not a ladder.

Carryover From Seattle

The reverse report graded the last start process-neutral (+0.23 net run value) wearing a 3-ER line. These were the watch items — here's where they stand entering tonight, and what each one is worth against this specific lineup.

1

Slider location: not fixed yet — and this is the lineup that punishes it. July zone rate on the slider against lefties is 51.7%, actually up from 48.2% before. The season-long math hasn't changed: 15% whiffs in the zone, 46% off the plate down-and-away. Tonight only two or three lefty bats start, so the reps are fewer — make every one finish off the plate. Aranda (25% whiff, disciplined) is the test case.

2

Changeup usage: trending right, not there yet. 18.4% against righties in July, up from 12.9% — and it struck out both circled victims in Seattle (Garver, Wilson). Tonight's lineup is the best changeup audience of the season. This is the start where 25–30% should finally print.

3

The cutter conversation stands. Thirty thrown in July at the season's worst damage rate (.486, a homer every 42). Tampa Bay adds two specific hazards: Yandy (.473 on cut/slide shapes at 94.9 EV) and DeLuca (.480). The recommendation is unchanged — shelve it.

4

Sinker command, not sinker identity. Both Seattle walks came on 3-2 sinkers. The pitch's job tonight is early-count weak contact against the slow flat bats — Palacios (.091 on sinkers), Simpson (47% chase), Vilade (.238). But it's hitter-specific now: Yandy (.443 at 97.9 EV), Caminero (.459 at 100.7) and DeLuca (.426) feast on it. The sinker has a whitelist tonight, not a green light.

Game Plan

The whole start in six calls.

Feature

The changeup leads tonight. Five of nine hitters have a changeup hole and the tunnel partner (ride four-seam) is already respected. 25–30% usage against righties — DeLuca, Fortes, Walls and Williamson should see it early and often, Yandy sees it dying off the away edge. This is the start where the season's best pitch finally gets a starter's workload.

Red Box

The Caminero rule: bury spin, take the walk, never challenge. He damages every zone pitch and chases exactly one shape — curveballs below the zone (44%). He also walks 17% of the time, so the door won't always open. That's fine. Three walks is a winning line. A zone fastball to the fastest bat in the sport is the only unforgivable pitch of the night.

Discipline

The flare-band defense is location, and it's stricter here than anywhere. Eight flat swings means zone mistakes become line drives at the highest rate on the schedule. The slider finishes off the plate (July habit says 52% zone — tonight's target is under 35%), the curve never catches white, the cutter stays in the pocket. Every soft pitch that leaks middle is a flare waiting to drop on turf.

Map

Fastball geography is a short list. Ladders: Walls (12° uphill), DeLuca (late), Mullins if he starts, Aranda at two strikes. Everyone else gets the four-seam on edges or above the swing only when ahead. The 2.8–3.2 shelf — the season's damage zone — is where five flat swings live. All the way up or somewhere else.

Whitelist

The sinker works for specific customers only. Green: Simpson (rollover side), Palacios (.091), Vilade early, Aranda first pitch. Banned: Yandy, Caminero, DeLuca — three of the four dangerous bats feast on arm-side sink. Early-count ground balls against the slow group keep the pitch count down for the at-bats that need craft.

Turf

Tropicana notes: fast turf, still air, short corners. Simpson's speed turns soft grounders into hits — against him, air contact is the win. The 315 line in left is Caminero and Vilade pull-side territory; nothing hard middle-in to either. No weather, no wind: pure execution night under the dome, and the catwalks reward keeping the ball out of the pull-side air.
