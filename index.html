The Briefing

Five things. If nothing else survives the trip up I-80, these do.

1

Jacob Wilson breaks the normal rules — plan for contact, not strikeouts. The two-hole hitter chases at the highest rates this series has ever graded — 47% on fastballs, 63% on changeups, 47% on slide-shape — and it doesn't matter, because he touches everything: eleven strikeouts in 113 trips, whiff rates in the teens. A 64.7 mph bat on a 6.2-foot swing means the chases become 78–86 EV dribblers, not misses. Expand to him for weak contact, never for the punchout, and never waste a 1-2 changeup expecting a swing-through that isn't coming.

2

The sinker minefield is the tightest of the season. Jonah Heim runs .571 expected damage on sinkers at 98.6 EV. Max Muncy is at .817 — the worst single matchup number in five reports. Henry Bolte averages 101.6 EV against them. Three hitters in the projected nine treat arm-side sink like batting practice. The whitelist tonight is short: Butler chasing (46%), White (.117), Walton off the bench — and that's about it.

3

Soderstrom is the lefty power problem — and the slider owns him. Nine homers against lefties, the most in this series, on a bat that sells out uphill (26° path at soft stuff). But he's whiffed on 41% of lefty slide-shape across 207 pitches — a huge, durable sample — and 64% of the few changeups lefties have dared throw him. The fixed slider gets its biggest assignment of the year: down, gloveside, repeatedly. His counter is the mistake: .402 when it hangs, in a park where hangs leave.

4

The right side is a ladder-and-tunnel harvest — with two exceptions. Bolte whiffs 47% on elevated fastballs and 57% on curves. Muncy misses 53% of elevated heat. Serven, White and Williams all chase soft stuff at 39–58%. The exceptions: Wilson (see card one) and Gelof if he starts — a hot .849 bat who punishes changeups (.505 at 94.9) but freezes on curves (50% whiff, 9% chase — a mini license candidate) and misses ride up (39%).

5

Sutter Health Park is the anti-Anaheim: small, hot, and honest about mistakes. A bandbox footprint, a 1:05 first pitch in August valley heat, and no marine layer to forgive elevated misses. The ladder plan still runs — but the "almost up" shelf that's been survivable at night in big parks is a souvenir stand here. Commit above the letters or stay on the edges. The park doesn't grade on a curve.

Carryover From Anaheim

The rematch delivered: 5.1 innings, one run, nine strikeouts, net run value −0.44 — eighteen punchouts over the last two starts. The scout's plans mostly cashed. The exceptions taught real lessons.

1

The changeup is no longer a watch item — it's the identity. 30% against righties in Anaheim, the fourth straight start on target, with four more strikeout finishes. Sixteen changeup putaways across four starts. It gets top billing again Sunday: White (58% chase), Heim (44%), Serven (46%), Bolte (37% whiff), Muncy (36%) are the customers. The one hard exception: Gelof (.505 damage — never).

2

The Trout homer, graded honestly: sound license, cashed anyway. The one run in Anaheim came on the zone-bottom curveball the card explicitly licensed — .112 season damage said it was the right bet, and he hit it 101.5 anyway. That's variance, not a broken process. But the same night, Peraza singled a non-licensed zone curve at .577 — the seventh straight start with a zone-curve receipt. The standing rule survives both data points: chase-only, no licenses issued this week (Gelof would be the only candidate, and only if he starts).

3

The cutter walked Mike Trout. One pitch, one receipt, three straight starts. Aranda's double, Williamson's HBP, now a free base to the best hitter alive — every cutter that has mattered since Seattle has produced damage. One thrown per start is one too many. The Sunday target remains zero, and the A's add Heim and Bolte as collectors-in-waiting.

4

The slider held its shape where it counted — and slipped a little where it didn't. 38% zone against lefties in Anaheim, up from San Francisco's 20% on a small sample. No damage resulted, but Sunday raises the stakes: Soderstrom's 41% whiff only exists below the zone, and his .402 on hangers is the counterweight. The 20% standard is the standard.

Game Plan

The whole start in six calls.

Holster

The sinker stays holstered against the heart of this lineup — no exceptions, no moments of faith. Muncy .817, Heim .571, Bolte 101.6 EV: three of the projected nine turn arm-side sink into slug. The green list (Butler chase, White, the lefty bench) is the whole list. Two straight starts of perfect whitelist discipline; this is the night it decides innings.

Slider

Soderstrom's four at-bats are the slider's season finale audition. 41% whiff on a 207-pitch sample — durable, real, waiting. Down and gloveside every time, at the San Francisco 20% zone standard, with the .402 hanger tax and a bandbox right field as the price of drift. Cortes and Walton off the bench extend the assignment.

Tunnel

The FF/CH tunnel harvests the right side — with the Wilson asterisk. Bolte, Muncy, White, Serven and Heim all miss or chase the fade; Muncy's 53% elevated whiff and Bolte's 47% make the ladder half of the tunnel just as live. Wilson chases everything and misses nothing: to him the expand is for weak contact and quick outs, never the strikeout. Four pitches max, defense does the rest.

Receipts

The standing bans, eighth verse: zero cutters, zero zone curves. The cutter has walked, plunked or doubled somebody in three straight starts — including Mike Trout on Monday. The zone curve has a receipt in seven straight. No licenses this week unless Gelof starts (his card carries the exception). In a park this small, the old mistakes cost double.

Heat Map

Day game in the valley: the ball carries, so the ladder is binary. Above the letters or on the black — the 2.8–3.2 shelf that Anaheim's marine layer forgave is a souvenir at 1 PM in Sacramento. Butler is the exception who can be challenged in the zone (78.6 EV on lefty heat); everyone else's zone fastballs are earned, not given.

Cash

Eighteen strikeouts in two starts — the budget here is Bolte, Muncy and the soft-stuff chasers. Bolte (37% K rate, 57% curve whiff), Muncy (53% up), White, Serven and Williams expanding: seven-plus strikeouts are sitting in the same vertical plan that's carried the last two starts. Wilson and McNeil are conceded to contact on purpose. Everyone gets a plan; nobody gets a mistake.
