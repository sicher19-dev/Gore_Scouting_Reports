The Briefing

Five things. If nothing else survives the South Side wind, these do.

1

Miguel Vargas is the toughest red box of the season — he has no pitch-type door. .984 against lefties with 12 homers and 28 walks, damage on every class (.557 fastball, .486 sinker, .492 slide-shape, .417 changeup), whiff rates in the low teens, and near-zero chase on hard stuff. The only behavioral opening: he expands at 38% of curves — the lone pitch he chases and his lowest damage (.384). This at-bat is played on the black, finished with buried curves, and conceded to first base without shame when the situation allows. Twelve homers say a strike he can reach is the worst outcome on the board.

2

Murakami is the marquee ladder harvest — 61.5% whiff on elevated four-seams, the highest number in seven reports — but he inverts the spin rule completely. A 37% strikeout rate against lefties built on heat he can't reach (41% whiff overall, 48% on slide-shape). And yet: 9% whiff on curves, 7% chase, .501 damage — he SEES spin down and punishes it. The season-long instinct to bury breaking balls gets one hitter's exemption in reverse: to Murakami it's ladders and sliders forever, curves never. Mistake tax: .542/.605 at 98–99 EV on anything hard he reaches.

3

Grichuk (1.098) is the career lefty-masher doing career lefty-masher things — and his door is the changeup. .340 with 9 homers and a .717 slug, but he chases 56% of changeups and whiffs on 35%. One giant caution: when a changeup catches plate he does .563 at 97.5 — this door only opens BELOW the zone. And never, ever spin: .771 on hangs, the single worst hanging-curve number of the series.

4

Colson Montgomery brings rare lefty-on-lefty power (9 HR) with the team's fastest bat — and three clean exits. 75.2 mph of bat speed, .838 OPS. The doors: changeup-shaped fades (42% whiff at 62 EV — devastating), curves (50% whiff), and the ladder (40% elevated). His swing steepens eight degrees hunting soft stuff and never finds it. The lefty menu runs three pitches deep; the only ban is anything hard and middle.

5

The bottom five is the softest of the series — harvest it cheaply and pitch the barbell honestly. Acuña (.476), Peters (.355), Rogers (69% changeup whiff, 63% slider whiff on the early sample), Antonacci (.589, 53% changeup chase), Doyle (.593, 41% whiff and 51% chase on changeups — but a .677/105 EV sinker landmine, the second-worst of the season). Five-plus strikeouts live down here on soft stuff alone, and every cheap out down low buys a free pass up top.

Game Plan

The whole start in six calls.

Barbell

Pitch the lineup like the barbell it is: honest combat 2 through 6, ruthless harvest everywhere else. Murakami, Vargas, Grichuk, Colson Montgomery and Doyle carry all the damage; Meidroth carries the on-base skill; the last three slots carry a combined OPS under .500. Cheap outs at the bottom (nine to twelve pitches for three outs is realistic) fund patient, edge-first at-bats at the top. Never let a bottom-third at-bat cost more than four pitches or a top-third at-bat get a middle-third pitch.

Toll Booth

The Vargas rule: buried curves are the only invitation he accepts — everything else is edges or a walk. No whiff door exists (.557/.486/.492/.417 across all classes). His 38% curve chase is the single behavior that ends at-bats on the pitcher's terms. Twelve homers in a homer park make the middle of the plate a forfeit; 28 walks make first base a fine outcome. Three disciplined at-bats, zero hero pitches.

Ladder

Murakami is the best strikeout matchup of the season — two pitches, zero exceptions. 61.5% whiff on elevated ride (a series record) plus 48% on sliders equals a two-pitch at-bat with four chances at the marquee strikeout. The exceptions ARE the plan: no curves ever (9% whiff, .501 — he inverts the arsenal's oldest rule), no sinkers ever (.605), and no fastball that stops climbing (.542 at 98 when he reaches it).

Fade

The changeup works both ends: Grichuk's door and the bottom-third harvest. The cleanup masher chases 56% of fades below the zone — the lone voluntary exit in a 1.098 season. Doyle (51%), Acuña (49%), Antonacci (53%) and Rogers (69% whiff) turn the bottom third into a fade festival, and Colson Montgomery's 42% whiff adds the rare lefty customer. Address discipline from the trilogy applies: below the knees, never at the thigh, and never in the zone to Grichuk (.563).

Gauges

First inning, two readings, then commit: velocity and slider depth. Normal rest for the first time since the fatigue flag — 95+ unlocks the full ladder plan; under 95 shifts weight to the fade-and-slider game the bottom third can't touch either way. Slider depth at 2.9 inches of ride is the eraser; near 5 is the flare. And the four-start middle-middle thread meets Vargas, Murakami and Grichuk in a Rate Field August — the release check-in isn't bookkeeping this week, it's the ballgame.

Park

Rate Field, late August: the ball leaves to all fields, and the wind decides how fast. The homer tax on middle-third mistakes is the highest of the season with 30 lefty-hit homers in the projected lineup's top five. The counterweight: the bottom third can't reach the seats at all, and the big foul territory rewards the ladder pop-ups. Same twin rules as the whole run — honest ladders, buried finish pitches — with less forgiveness than anywhere yet.
