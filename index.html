The Briefing

Five things. The lineup and the blueprint agree; the only question is whether the plan survives the first inning.

1

This lineup does not miss fastballs in the zone — and misses almost everything soft below the knees. Perdomo whiffs on 2.9% of four-seams, Arenado 6.4%, Marte and Ildemaro Vargas 10.7%, Moreno 11.6%. Elevated fastballs get nothing (Perdomo 4%, Arenado 9%, Marte 15%). But soft pitches under 1.6 feet produce whiffs of 53–62% from Marte, Arenado, Tawa, Waldschmidt, Lawlar and Carroll. It's the Rays lineup's contact profile with a far bigger chase habit — and the height thread from the last reverse (sinkers at the knees, changeups below the thigh, curves never mid-zone) isn't a correction here. It's the entire strikeout plan.

2

Ketel Marte is the red box — the rare one with a door. A 77.8 mph bat (second-fastest graded this series), .931 with nine homers, and in-zone damage that bans three pitches outright: sinkers (.565 at 95.7), curves (.489 at 101.5), changeups in the zone (.493 at 97.8). Unlike Vargas or Caminero, he expands: 42% chase on fastballs off the plate, 51% on sliders, and a 55% whiff rate on anything soft below 1.6 feet. Nothing in the zone, ever — the at-bat is fastballs just off the edge, sliders off the plate, and fades in the dirt. He'll swing at them.

3

Arenado is the sinker's greenest name of the season (.159) — and the fastball's most dangerous customer. Zone four-seams are his only food: .472 with a 6.4% whiff rate and no ladder (9% up on a 13.4° uppercut). Everything else beats him: sinkers (.159 at 87 EV), sliders (34% whiff, .185), curves (35%, .312), changeups (31%), and a 53% whiff rate below the knees. The 8.4-foot swing is built for one pitch shape; the plan withholds it entirely.

4

Corbin Carroll (.942) is a lineup question — subbed in on the 7th, sat the 8th — and if he plays, he's a rare left-handed changeup customer. .272 on changeups with a 32% whiff and 35% chase; .313 on sliders. The ladder is a coin flip: 50% whiff on elevated heat, but .473 at 96.9 when he reaches it — two strikes only. Curves are banned (.510 at 99). Verify his status Saturday; the card is ready either way.

5

The bottom half chases like the Rays' did — the walk-thread fix continues here. Ildemaro Vargas expands at 41% (49% on fastballs, 52% on changeups) with five walks in 154 trips. Gurriel chases 45% and swings at 42% of first pitches. Moreno 40% on sliders. The patient ones are the switch-hitting leadoff (Perdomo, 24 walks, 14.5% first-pitch swing) and Waldschmidt (18.5% chase). Everyone in between will swing at pitches that end at-bats off the plate.

Carryover From The Rays Start

3 innings, 5 runs, the worst process grade of the series — diagnosed as the plan first, then the arm. What travels to Phoenix:

1

The sequencing rule is in the gamebar now, not the briefing. The Rays scout said soft-first at 94.5 and the afternoon went 66% fastballs and two curves. The A's start — 20% curves, 19% changeups, one middle fastball in 95 pitches — is the proof it works at this velocity. Against a lineup with a 2.9%-whiff leadoff hitter and a 6.4%-whiff Arenado, the fastball in the zone is a location pitch and nothing more. Breaking balls and fades are first-pitch options. The fastball earns its share by where it goes, not by habit.

2

Six days of rest — the structural ask, granted. Three straight starts under the band, in-game decay to 93.2 last time, the ride down to 15.6 inches. The last six-day gap (before the trilogy closer) produced 95.4 in the first and the best process night of the season. The gauge gets read as information: 95+ is a bonus that widens the ladder assignments; 94 is the setting the whole card is already written for. Either way, in-game decay after 40 pitches is the shorten-the-outing trigger, and it fires without argument.

3

Height is the thread — and here it's also the weapon. Five of six fixable marks in the Rays start were vertical: sinkers at the top of the zone, a changeup at the letters (third receipt), a curve at mid-zone. This lineup punishes the same mistakes (Marte .493 on zone changeups, Moreno .508 on sinkers that catch plate) and rewards the fix more than any yet: 53–62% whiffs below the knees up and down the order. Green-list sinkers at the knees. Fades below the thigh. Curves buried. The in-game trigger stands: two elevated soft pitches in an inning is the alarm.

4

What travels intact: four straight homerless starts (25 innings), the retired middle-middle thread, zero cutters, and the whitelist by name at seven straight. The bans are championship-grade and have held through a homer park, a barbell lineup, and two rematches. Chase Field's humidor and deep alleys reward the discipline. Stuff tracking continues: the slider has arrived as a 0.3–0.9 inch sweeper the last two starts (season 2.9) and the ride is at 15.4–15.6 — fatigue signatures, checked in the first inning, off the list when velocity returns.

Game Plan

The whole start in six calls — the first one is in the gamebar, on purpose.

Sequence

Soft-first, from pitch one, as a rule and not a preference. This lineup doesn't miss zone fastballs (Perdomo 2.9%, Arenado 6.4%, Marte 10.7%) and whiffs at 53–62% below the knees. The A's start proved the blueprint at this velocity; the Rays start proved what abandoning it costs. Breaking balls and fades open at-bats. The fastball lives on edges and just off them, where five hitters chase it at 30–49%. Six days of rest may bring velocity back; it changes the ladder list (Carroll, Lawlar, the lefty bench), not the sequencing rule.

Marte

The red box has a door, and the door is the strike zone's edge — nothing inside it, ever. .565 sinkers, .493 changeups, .489 curves, .459 sliders in the zone, at 95–101 EV, from a 77.8 mph bat. But 42% chase on fastballs off the plate, 51% on sliders, 55% whiff on soft stuff below 1.6 feet. Four at-bats of fastballs an inch off, sliders a foot off, and fades in the dirt. He'll swing; let him. The walk is a fine outcome; the strike he reaches is the only bad one.

Arenado

Withhold his one food and feed him the rest — the sinker at the knees is the surprise weapon. Zone four-seams .472 at a 6.4% whiff; everything else beats the 8.4-foot swing: sinkers .159, sliders .185 (34% whiff), curves 35%, changeups 31%, 53% below the knees. The greenest sinker matchup of the season, with the height caveat from last start attached: at the knees, or it's a line drive.

Height

The thread is the weapon: every soft pitch finishes below the knees, every green-list sinker at the knees. Five of six fixable marks last start were vertical. This lineup's low-soft whiff rates — Tawa 62%, Waldschmidt 61%, Carroll 59%, Marte 55%, Lawlar 55%, Arenado 53% — are the reward for fixing it; Tawa's .406 and Marte's .493 on zone changeups are the punishment for not. Two elevated soft pitches in an inning is the in-game alarm, and it shortens the outing.

Counts

The bottom half chases; the top half takes — spend the strikes accordingly. Ildemaro Vargas (41% chase, 5 BB in 154), Gurriel (45%), Moreno on soft (34–41%), Marte (38%) end at-bats off the plate. Perdomo (24 BB, 14.5% first-pitch swing) and Waldschmidt (18.5% chase) don't — those two get strike one in the zone and the curve/fade as the finisher. Eight walks in two starts became three last time; the free bases live only at 1 and 9 here.

Park

Chase Field: humidor, roof, and the deepest alleys in the division — the discipline's best friend. Four straight homerless starts and a retired middle-middle thread meet a park that turns warning-track contact into outs. The counterweight is the contact profile: this lineup puts the ball in play, so the defense works and the ground-ball soft stuff (Arenado, Perdomo, Waldschmidt sinkers at the knees) matters more than the radar. The first-inning gauge is information; the gamebar rule is the plan. Six days of rest, a fresh opponent, and a lineup that swings at the plan's best pitches — go execute it.
